SUBJECT: Submission of Regular Research Article: “Anytime-Valid Recalibration of Symmetric Quantum Learners from Free Symmetry Syndromes under Hardware Drift”

Dear Editors of Quantum,

We are pleased to submit our original research manuscript entitled “Anytime-Valid Recalibration of Symmetric Quantum Learners from Free Symmetry Syndromes under Hardware Drift” for consideration as a Regular Research Article in Quantum.

Physical hardware drift—manifesting as telegraphic two-level system (TLS) defect frequency jumps, stochastic local-oscillator phase drift, and energy relaxation decays—presents a major bottleneck for quantum processing units (QPUs) deployed on commercial clouds. Current industry practice relies on rigid, periodic recalibration schedules (typically every 8 to 24 hours), causing up to 80% budget wastage on stable days and silent algorithmic failure during mid-cycle defect jumps. Meanwhile, contemporary drift benchmarking proposals (such as spectator qubits or randomized benchmarking interspersal) require dedicated quantum circuits, consuming 25% to 50% extra quantum shots.

In this manuscript, we present SymSentinel, a framework that completely eliminates the shot overhead of quantum hardware monitoring while providing rigorous mathematical guarantees:

  • Zero Extra Quantum Shot Cost (“Free Sentinels”): We prove that for any symmetric quantum learner ($[U(\theta), S] = 0$), computational basis shots gathered for task observable evaluation inherently carry hardware drift syndromes. These syndromes are evaluated classically in microseconds at zero extra quantum shot cost.
  • Filtration-Predictable Betting E-Detectors (Theorem 1): We construct a sequential test martingale with strictly predictable betting fractions ($\lambda_t \in \mathcal{F}_{t-1}$) guaranteeing non-asymptotic Average Run Length control ($\mathrm{ARL} \ge \gamma$) via Ville's maximal inequality. Over 1,000 independent null runs, our detector achieved zero false alarms.
  • The Blind-Spot Visibility Limit Theorem (Theorem 2): We prove that a single symmetry sentinel $S$ is strictly blind to noise channels whose Kraus operators commute with $S$ ($[E_k, S] = 0$), even when task prediction risk degrades severely. We resolve this via a greedy set-cover algorithm over a sentinel library that restores 100% full Pauli fault visibility with just $M=2$ sentinels.
  • Anytime-Valid Conformal Risk Certification (Theorem 3): Combining an empirical-Bernstein confidence sequence on validation data with an isotonic link function, we construct an upper risk certificate $U_t^{\mathrm{cert}}$ guaranteeing time-uniform coverage ($\le 0.05$ miscoverage) across non-stationary drift regimes ($D_1$ coherent OU, $D_2$ relaxation, and $D_3$ TLS jumps).
  • Three-Tier Recalibration Hierarchy & 96.6% Cloud Cost Reduction (Theorem 4): Rather than triggering monolithic $100 full QPU resets upon every alarm, SymSentinel deploys a 3-tier cascade: software Virtual-$Z$ phase updates ($0, 0 shots) $\to$ localized 2-qubit subsystem retuning ($15, 1.5k shots) $\to$ full QPU reset. In comprehensive benchmarking across 6 baselines, SymSentinel achieves 96.6% cost reduction compared to periodic recalibration and 82.9% reduction compared to an omniscient oracle reset.

Given Quantum's leadership in quantum error mitigation, quantum machine learning, and reproducible open science, we believe our work will be of immediate interest to your readership.
